# FloorKit Phase 0 threat model

Scope: independent proposed coin instances, local model only. No FloorKit account or contract is deployed. Mathematical safety assumes the approved transition API, checked arithmetic and authenticated runtime inputs. Runtime failure, administrator control and liveness are separate gates.

## Assets, actors and privileges

Protect R, token balances, earned creator credit F, available claims C, unresolved native attempts I, free/allocated storage H/K and protected operations O. Never count future fees, external prices, Floorline backing or unreturned transfers as new backing.

Users may mint, redeem their available coins, transfer, fund their own/others' storage, withdraw their own fixed-beneficiary claims, and request known-failure restoration. A creator's only ongoing financial privilege is converting **already earned F** into a claim to their immutable account ID. A deployer selects bounded initial parameters and bootstrap allocations once. No creator/deployer/DAO is allowed to change tax/markup/fees, force transfer, reserve-drain, mint arbitrary units, redirect claims, sweep U/O or reopen closure.

Callbacks have privileged *runtime* authority, not human authority. `#[private]`, actual promise-result count/type and an exact stored nonce are required. The pure model uses SELF and trusted `env_*` inputs to test ordering; caller-supplied success/refund booleans must never be exposed by a future contract. [NEAR private callback guidance](https://docs.near.org/smart-contracts/security/cross-contract-calls/callbacks).

## Economic attacks

- **Zero-supply capture/reset:** a naive flat-tax example R=T=100, τ=20%, all tokens redeemed leaves 20 reserve. Minting one new token against a fresh initial price can let the new holder capture the old 20; restarting from R=0 can also reset a previously higher B. Chosen mitigation: full-curve exit pays all R, then permanent closure. Direct native receipts remain U. No bootstrap/reset method exists after initialization.
- **Fee extraction:** R=T=100,d=100,m=100,f=5 produces B=195/200 if gross d priced the mint. Mitigation: subtract f before pricing m; segregate F at deposit; withdraw only earned credit. B is independent of fee-claim payment.
- **Rounding exploits:** floor f may make small split buys avoid part of creator fee; floor m/q can retain dust for remaining holders. No exact-fee-under-splitting promise. Reject zero mint/payout, use one final curve division, full-width multiplication and checked narrowing. The direct mint/redeem inequality q+f<=d rules out a rounding-only immediate round-trip profit, including buyer=creator; it does not cover intervening donations/trades.
- **Dust:** no burn for zero-output q. A minimum of one atomic native unit per atomic token is not promised. Token dust can keep an instance active until holders aggregate enough tokens; there is no forced terminal burn or dust confiscation.
- **Stale quotes/front-running:** immutable markup is not a fixed quote. Use exact min_tokens/min_near and U64 deadline from chain time. A quote can become invalid; rejects roll back. Sandwiching, block ordering, quote-provider dishonesty and external market execution are not eliminated.
- **False redemption-floor claims:** R=T=10000,c=1000,τ=2000 gives q=820. A fee-free 1% markup buy d=10000 produces m=9900, B'=20000/19900>1, yet q'=814. The accounting claim still holds; a fixed-redemption-quote monotonicity claim is disproven. Do not relabel the curve payout as a guaranteed floor.
- **Reserve drains:** no admin reserve withdrawal, loans, routers spending R, or balance-minus-liabilities sweep. Redemption replaces reserve with a funded claim. Creator credit cannot contain old reserve. Full exits cannot take H/K/O/F or another holder's claims.
- **Malicious parameters:** hard caps, positive p, canonical metadata limits, fixed decimals and immutable beneficiary. Reject low-p or large-seed supply overflow and bootstrap rounding to zero. High but allowed taxes/markup can be economically unattractive; mathematical soundness does not mean good price/fair distribution. Bootstrap recipient/seed allocation must be public.
- **Sybil/wash activity:** transfers create no R increase; trades pay real deposit/fees/rounding. Sybil volume is not community demand or independent human participation. Creator/self buyers cannot derive immediate mint/redeem cash profit solely from their own fee, but can manipulate social volume and ownership concentration. No rewards, leaderboard subsidies or fake-live metrics are proposed.

## Async, storage and operational attacks

- **Double withdrawal:** Available→InFlight occurs before scheduling; only one live nonce. Paid, Failed or unresolved claims cannot dispatch directly. Repeated/stale/wrong-owner callbacks reject. A separate failed restoration is required before a fresh nonce.
- **Overlapping promises:** A-P includes undebited promises. Never reclassify this difference into R/F/O or accept it alone as proof of a returned refund. Restoration requires known failure plus A>=P+I; INVARIANTS.md proves the conservative cushion rule, including delayed old refunds and callbacks before/after refund arrival.
- **Unknown callback:** delivered transfer plus missing callback remains InFlight forever. A timeout, transaction parent success or purported explorer screenshot cannot authorize another payout. This can strand bookkeeping/rights; solvency does not prove payout liveness. A future recovery mechanism requires a separately reviewed protocol, not an admin patch hidden in this design.
- **Malicious receiver/FT callback:** cap unused refund by original amount and receiver's remaining tokens. Sender and receiver remain registered while callbacks are pending. A receiver can forward/redeem tokens, so original tokens are not magically restored. Resolver never burns a missing sender balance. Protect settlement from duplicate resolution and callback expansion.
- **Storage exhaustion:** every growing record is funded before allocation; callbacks use fixed-width preallocated fields. Limit metadata/memo/message sizes. Unknown callbacks may occupy slots indefinitely. The finite operations-funded refund pool avoids unbounded unfunded refunds, but can halt new excess-refund/unregister operations until cleared. Explicitly test pool saturation and known-failure recovery; never allocate a free fallback row.
- **Force unregister:** this profile rejects destruction of live token/claim/FT-callback rights, including `force=true`. Unregistered-account and return-shape details require the adapter cases in the architecture. The standard permits refusing unsupported forced unregistration; this is not unrestricted force-burn support. [NEP-145](https://github.com/near/NEPs/blob/master/neps/nep-0145.md).
- **Native direct transfers/rewards:** remain U, not automatic R or earned F. Only explicit attached contributions increase backing. No public sweep. This intentionally may trap unsolicited cash after closure. Native coin balance and credited reserve must remain visibly different.
- **Gas/operations:** original caller funds execution/callback gas. No controller signs contract-funded gas transactions after lock-down. O is code/storage/refund headroom, not an unbounded gas sponsor or issuer revenue. No O-spending API is admitted. Actual costs and any new outflow need runtime proof. Network protocol/storage-price changes are outside an unconditional forever-economic guarantee.
- **Arithmetic:** U128 amounts/aggregates and U64 counters are checked; multiplication is U512. Do not narrow numerator midway. Reject JSON negative/exponent/oversized IDs or noncanonical amounts in the adapter. Counter exhaustion freezes affected creation safely. Boundary environment events cannot create an account balance above protocol U128 capacity.

Storage staking is an encumbrance within native balance; measure serialized usage using the live protocol byte cost, never pay for it out of R. The pure model's small storage constants only exercise accounting. [NEAR storage documentation](https://docs.near.org/protocol/storage/storage-staking).

## Zero access keys is not enough

Full-access keys can transfer native funds, replace code and manage keys, so retaining one invalidates an adversarial-key-holder economic guarantee. Zero access keys removes that external signing path; users can still invoke the deployed contract. [NEAR access keys](https://docs.near.org/protocol/accounts-contracts/access-keys).

A contract can itself create deploy-code/key-management/native-transfer actions. Removing keys does not disable an exported self-upgrade, arbitrary Promise/action executor, delegated admin, migration entrypoint, hidden reserve spender or code path that restores a key. Audit generated Wasm exports and the complete reachable source/action graph; an unaudited no-keys account is not automatically immutable. [Programmatic upgrade documentation](https://docs.near.org/smart-contracts/release/upgrade).

Also audit code references. A global contract referenced by publisher account ID follows publisher updates, even if the coin has no local keys. A hash reference pins code; a regular local-code deployment is simpler for this deadline. Exclude mutable global aliases and proxy/dispatcher dependencies. [NEAR global contracts](https://docs.near.org/smart-contracts/global-contracts).

Proposed locked-down policy: regular local Wasm, atomic initialization, no exported upgrade/migrate/admin/action-execution/restore-key/delete-account paths, no privileged delegated callers, independently matched code hash and public zero-key evidence **only after** runtime gates and separate deployment authorization. This phase performs none of those on-chain actions. Existing Floorline access keys remain retained and untouched; it remains mutable. Protocol governance/bugs, compiler/runtime correctness and future network changes are not eliminated by this policy.

## Stop conditions

Stop on any economic invariant failure and preserve exact seed/trace; do not change B's definition to make it disappear. Stop implementation/release on an unproven async debit/refund assumption, unfunded storage record, native/FT double-settlement, mutable economics, unreviewed key/global upgrade path, ordinary-call gas growth with history, or missing budget/signing authorization. Passing this pure model is not permission to deploy or a security audit.
