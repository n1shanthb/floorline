# Floorline external-loan salvage decision
November 10, 2026 · Asia/Calcutta · architecture review only

**Recommendation: conditional GO for this architecture; NO-GO for committing to a three-track November 14 delivery today.** The external-loan variant has no identified mathematical contradiction. The complete implementation, deployment and three-track eligibility remain unverified. Do not build the alternative until this architecture is accepted. Do not ship a two-track fallback if the NFT or DAO demonstration fails.

## 1. Can the treasury guarantee be defensible? Yes, precisely limited.

Define L as unencumbered native NEAR redemption backing and T as total outstanding token units, including every locked collateral token. Pending free/reserved issuance must either be prohibited or included once as a funded claim. B=L/T for T>0 is a backing ratio, not a guaranteed per-token redemption payout, market price or USD floor.

For issuance of m units adding d **net** NEAR to backing, require d*T>=L*m. Fix the tax at 0<=t<1. A redemption of c consumes only unlocked tokens and pays a checked, downward-rounded version of L*c/T*(1-t+t*c/T), with min_out. Since payout<=L*c/T, remaining B cannot fall. Full redemption makes B undefined. Initial issuance establishes B; it does not increase a pre-existing ratio. No zero-cost reserved issuance, backing distributions, treasury loans, or storage subsidies are allowed. DAO gifts increase L. Storage and operational reserves are separately funded.

External funding, collateral locking, NFT transfer, repayment and default change neither L nor T. Default transfers tokens to the lender; it does not burn or mint them. These operations therefore preserve B exactly. A subsequent lender redemption satisfies the ordinary redemption inequality. Locked collateral can temporarily prevent total exit, so do not promise all token units are redeemable while locked. Integer dust can still produce zero quotes; no positive universal minimum payout is proven.

Your original formula is now recorded: S=surplus+total_borrowed; N=token_supply+pending_reserved+total_collateral; R(c)=min(liquid_surplus,S*c/N*(1-t+t*c/N)). It resembles nominal accounting in [REVLoans](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rev-net/revnet-core-v6/main/src/REVLoans.sol) and [REVOwner](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rev-net/revnet-core-v6/main/src/REVOwner.sol), which burn collateral and account for possible reminting. It is not validated as an equivalent port. With locked collateral already included in token_supply, adding total_collateral again double counts claims. External principal is owed to the lender, not the treasury, and must never enter treasury surplus or total_borrowed. pending_reserved needs an exact definition to avoid another double count. The liquid cap limits a payment; it does not establish monotonicity. For this salvage, use L and T directly, with no treasury loan receivables.

## 2. Do externally funded loans preserve genuine NFT utility? Yes.

The NFT transfers an exclusive, expiring right to repay a fixed debt and receive a fixed quantity of locked tokens. Carol locks c tokens; a lender supplies p NEAR separately; Carol receives a fully cash-backed payout claim and the loan-right NFT. Carol transfers it to Dave. Dave pays exactly fixed debt r>=p before expiry; the lender receives a fully funded claim for r and Dave receives c tokens. At expiry, the lender receives c tokens and the NFT right terminates. The lender's identity and terms remain fixed when the borrower-right NFT transfers. The NFT is a right, not a mechanism for forcing personal debt onto its recipient.

“Fully escrowed” must be stated correctly: principal is cash escrowed **before origination**, then released to the borrower. After release it is a nonrecourse collateralized loan, not a principal-guaranteed investment. The lender bears collateral price/liquidity risk. No promised NEAR recovery, oracle, forced sale or automatic liquidation proceeds. Accrued debt must be fixed in advance; never create an unfunded withdrawal claim for interest. Interest becomes payable only when the borrower actually attaches repayment.

Minimal architecture: one bounded contract with a treasury ledger, internal fungible-token balances, locked balances, lender funding offers, loan positions and standard loan NFTs. Internal collateral movement avoids cross-contract FT escrow races. No secondary market UI, partial repayment, refinancing, variable interest or transferable lender claims. Standard-compliant NFT transfer_call and its resolver remain required; see [NEP-171](https://nomicon.io/Standards/Tokens/NonFungibleToken/Core). An unresolved transfer_call blocks repayment/default settlement; callbacks must be private, bounded and idempotent. No unsafe timeout-based assumption that transfer failed.

State: FundedOffer -> Active -> Repaid OR Defaulted; cancellation only before acceptance. Origination atomically consumes a funded offer, locks collateral, creates the NFT and reserves the borrower's payout. Repayment atomically consumes attached repayment, closes the NFT, credits the lender and unlocks to the current NFT owner. Default at now>=expiry credits collateral to the lender once. Repayment requires now<expiry. External NEAR withdrawals use a separate persistent Reserved/InFlight/Paid/RefundConfirmed state machine; no immediate re-spend on an unknown callback outcome.

Cash invariant at receipt boundaries: local actual NEAR >= storage/operations + L + unused lender funding + locally held payout claims + storage-refund liabilities. In-flight transfers are separately tracked and excluded from local spendable cash. Principal receivables and collateral values are never added to this cash equation. Every payout claim is created by moving already-held cash or consuming an attached deposit, never by booking future repayment. Collateral invariant: sum of active loan collateral equals locked token balances; available+locked balances sum to T. These are proof obligations for the future implementation, not results established by the old tests.
